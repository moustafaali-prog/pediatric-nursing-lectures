1Oxygen Therapy

Oxygen Therapy

Dr. Moustafa Al-Shammari

2Learning Objectives

Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, the student nurse will be able to:

Define oxygen therapy.

List the purposes of oxygen therapy.

Identify indications of oxygen therapy.

List methods of oxygen therapy.

Describe the equipment required.

Perform appropriate techniques for each method.

3Introduction

Introduction

Oxygen is an essential body requirement for any energy consumption, activity or function. Hypoxemia resulting from apnea or inadequate ventilation and it occurs more rapidly in children than in adults because of children's higher metabolic rate and increase oxygen consumption.

Oxygen therapy is a treatment for acute or chronic respiratory conditions or ventilation failure, often lifesaving.

4Purposes

Purposes

Prevent hypoxemia.

Ensure tissue oxygenation in conditions such as:

Reduced oxygen-carrying capacity (e.g., anemia).

Decreased cardiac output.

Increased metabolism.

5Indications

Indications

Oxygen therapy is indicated in:

Decreased environmental oxygen (e.g., inhalation of non-physical gas mixtures).

Impaired alveolar ventilation (e.g., severe airway obstruction or lung pathology).

Decreased oxygen-carrying capacity (e.g., acute anemia).

Abnormal pulmonary blood flow: (Persistent fetal circulation, Acute pulmonary hypertension)

Poor tissue perfusion (e.g., shock states).

Acute brain insults (e.g., increased intracranial pressure).

6Types of Oxygen Therapy

Types of Oxygen Therapy

High Flow Rate:

13-15L/min, 60-80%.

Methods: Tent, Ambo bag.

Low Flow Rate:

1-13L/min, 40-60%.

Methods: Nasal cannula, face mask, etc.

7Methods

Methods

1. Simple Face Mask

Advantages:

Variety of Sizes: Available in various sizes to fit different ages and facial structures.

Universal Fit: Suitable for all age groups.

Oxygen Flow Rate: Can deliver an oxygen flow rate of 5-10 L/min.

Predictable Concentration: Delivers a consistent oxygen concentration even if the child breathes through the nose or mouth.

Disadvantages:

Tolerability: Some children may not tolerate wearing the mask.

Short-Term Use: Not ideal for long-term oxygen therapy.

Concentration Dependence: The effectiveness of oxygen delivery depends on how securely the mask fits.

Disruptions: Activities such as eating or talking can disrupt the oxygen supply.

8Methods

Methods

2. Nasal Cannula

Advantages:

Age Versatility: Suitable for all age groups.

Mobility: Allows the child considerable freedom of movement.

Visual Field Uninterrupted: Does not obstruct the child's view.

Constant O2 Flow: Provides continuous oxygen even when the child eats or talks.

Flow Rate: Can deliver oxygen at a rate of 1-5 L/min.

Disadvantages:

Resistance: Young children may resist wearing the cannula.

Irritation: Can irritate the nasal mucosa.

Restraint Need: May require restraining the child to keep it in place.

Concentration Control: Difficult to control the oxygen concentration if the child breathes through the mouth.

— ‪NASAL OXYGEN CANNULA, 2 prongs + tube, adult | Unicat‬‏

9Methods

Methods

Head Box (Hood)

Advantages:

Size Variety: Available in different sizes for different patient needs.

Flow Rate: Maximum flow rate of up to 10 L/min.

Unrestricted Movement: Does not limit facial or head movement.

Disadvantages:

Limited Access: Makes it challenging to access the patient's head for examination or treatment.

Humidity: Creates a high-humidity environment that can be uncomfortable.

Interruption During Care: Must be removed during feeding and while providing patient care

— ‪Rilekh Oxygen Hood for Newborn baby, Pediatric : Amazon.in: Baby Products‬‏

10Methods

Methods

Mist tent

Advantages:Tent allows older children considerable mobility.Maximum flow rate (10L/m).

Disadvantages:Steady O2 concentration difficult to maintain.Child feel isolated.Cool and wet tent environment.Poor access to patient.

— Croup Tent: A Comprehensive Overview (2024)

11Methods

Methods

Incubator

Advantages:

Neonatal Use: Suitable only for neonates and young infants.

Mobility: Infant mobility is not restricted.

Observation and Access: Allows easy observation and assessment of the infant and makes interventions straightforward.

Disadvantages:

Oxygen Concentration Limitation: To achieve oxygen concentrations higher than 85%, an oxygen hood must be placed inside the incubator.

— ‪Why do Babies Need Infant Incubators? - Heal Force Group‬‏

12Equipment

Equipment

. O2 source (cylinder or central piping system).

· Delivery equipment.

· Oxygen flowmeter.

· Oxygen tubing.

· Humidification attachment.

· Disposable gloves.

· Water soluble lubricant (for catheter insertion)

— ‪Flowmeter w/ Humidifier, Cage Mounted, Male DISS Fitting - Vet Supplies‬‏

13Humidification

Humidification

Purpose of Humidification:

Prevents drying of respiratory mucosa.

Loosens secretions.

Prevents dehydration of mucous membranes.

14Procedure for Oxygen Therapy

Procedure for Oxygen Therapy

Perform hand hygiene.

Select appropriate equipment.

Ensure proper humidification to prevent dryness.

Use sterile water for bacterial prevention.

Attach and position the delivery device (e.g., cannula, mask, hood).

Monitor the oxygen flow and adjust as prescribed.

Evaluate the child’s response through SaO₂ levels or arterial blood gases.

15Technique Notes

Technique Notes

Specific Considerations:

For nasal cannula: Insert prongs properly and encourage nasal breathing.

For mask: Secure straps snugly.

For hood: Ensure comfort and avoid resting hood on the neck.

16Evaluation of Response

Evaluation of Response

Assess Effectiveness Through:

Pulse Oximeter (SaO2): Target above 90%.

Arterial Blood Gas (PaO2): Target above 90 mmHg.

Poor Response Indicators:

SaO2 under 85%.

PaO2 under 60 mmHg.
