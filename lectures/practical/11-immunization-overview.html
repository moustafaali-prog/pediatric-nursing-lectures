4Vaccines

Vaccines

Vaccine: A suspension of live or killed microorganisms or their antigenic portions, used to induce immunity to specific diseases.

Toxoid: A modified bacterial toxin used to induce immunity against toxins (e.g., tetanus, diphtheria).

5Types of Vaccines:

Types of Vaccines:

Live Attenuated: Contains weakened live viruses (e.g., Sabin Polio, Measles, Mumps).

Inactivated or Killed: Contains dead viruses or bacteria (e.g., Salk Polio, Rabies).

Cellular Fraction: Contains fragments of bacteria (e.g., Pneumococcal, Meningococcal).

6Types of Vaccines:

Types of Vaccines:

Recombinant: Contain a genetically engineered version of the virus (e.g., Hepatitis B).

Toxoids: Modified toxins (e.g., Tetanus, Diphtheria).

Immunoglobulin: Contains antibodies (e.g., Hepatoglobulin).

Antitoxins: Contains antibodies to neutralize toxins (e.g., Tetaglobulin).

10Polio Vaccines - Types

Polio Vaccines - Types

Sabin (Oral) Vaccine:

Live attenuated virus.

Mass immunization.

Easier to administer.

Potential community-wide immunity.

11Polio Vaccines - Types

Polio Vaccines - Types

Salk (Inactivated) Vaccine:

Injected.

Longer lifespan than Sabin.

Effective in preventing polio but less suited for mass vaccination.

12DPT Vaccine and Side Effects

DPT Vaccine and Side Effects

DPT: Diphtheria, Pertussis, Tetanus.

Side Effects:

Mild: Local swelling, slight fever.

Moderate: Shock.

Severe: Convulsions, encephalopathy (rare).

Note: If severe reaction occurs, use D.T vaccine instead of DPT.

13MMR Vaccine and Side Effects

MMR Vaccine and Side Effects

Live Attenuated (Measles, Mumps, Rubella).

Side Effects:

Fever and rash (5-15%).

Transient arthritis (rare).

Encephalopathy (very rare).

Contraindications: Pregnancy, immunosuppression, severe allergic reactions.

14Hepatitis B Vaccine and Side Effects

Hepatitis B Vaccine and Side Effects

Common Side Effects:

Erythema, induration at injection site.

Fever, malaise, flu-like symptoms.

Rare: Arthritis, myalgia.

15Rota Virus Vaccine

Rota Virus Vaccine

Side Effects:

Diarrhea, vomiting, nasopharyngitis, otitis media.

Contraindications: Immunocompromised children should not receive live vaccines due to the risk of adverse reactions from the live virus.

16Immunization for Immunocompromised Children

Immunization for Immunocompromised Children

Contraindicated Vaccines: Live bacterial or viral vaccines (e.g., MMR, rotavirus).

Postponing Vaccination: Wait 3 months after immunosuppressive treatment.

17Pre-Vaccination Assessment

Pre-Vaccination Assessment

Questions to Ask:

Is the child sick today?

Any severe allergies or past reactions to vaccines?

Weakened immune system?

Recent blood transfusion?

Developmental concerns?

Important: Minor illnesses like a cold or mild diarrhea are not contraindications.

18Nursing Actions Before Vaccine Administration

Nursing Actions Before Vaccine Administration

General Instructions:

Ensure the child is healthy and doesn’t have contraindications.

Provide clear explanations to parents.

Administer the vaccine following correct procedures.

19Potential Reactions and Nursing Interventions

Potential Reactions and Nursing Interventions

Pain with Injection:

Use topical anesthetics and distractions (e.g., bubbles, music).

Fever/Myalgia:

Administer acetaminophen.

Severe Reactions:

Monitor for allergic reactions, seizures, and provide emergency care as necessary.

20Common Questions Parents May Ask

Common Questions Parents May Ask

— Eye dropper

What are immunizations? Vaccines stimulate the body to defend against infections.

Are immunizations safe? Most vaccines are safe; side effects are generally mild and temporary.

— Eye dropper
