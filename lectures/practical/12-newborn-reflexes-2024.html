3Page 3

Reflexes

Testing reflexes is an important part of the

neurologic examination.

Reflexes should be tested at the end of the

examination because they may disturb the

infant and interfere with auscultation.

Reflexes are present at birth and disappear by

9 months of age.

4Page 4

Classification of reflexes

• Primitive reflexes:

A- feeding reflexes: sucking, rooting, and

swallowing reflex

B- Motor reflex: like Moro , startle , palmer,

planter,…etc.

• Protective reflexes: Gag, cough, and sneeze

reflexes also are present in newborns to

maintain a clear airway.

5Page 5

Primitive reflexes originate in the central nervous system and

are exhibited in normal infants

• Rooting reflex: The infant turns his head toward the

mother’s breast and begins to suck when his lower

lip or cheek is stroked. should disappear at about age

3-4 months but may persist for up to 12 months.

6Page 6

• Sucking reflex: The infant moves his lips, mouth, and

tongue and shows interest to feed. Present at birth;

disappears at 10–12 months.

• Startle

reflexes:

Sudden

loud

noise

causes

abduction of arms with flexion of elbows; hands

remain clenched; disappears by age 4 months

8Page 8

•

Tonic neck reflex (or fencing posture): The infant

turns his head to one side, and the arm on that side

straightens while the opposite arm bends in a fencing

position.

9Page 9

• Moro reflex: Sudden jarring or change in

equilibrium causes sudden extension and

abduction of extremities and fanning of fingers,

with index finger and thumb forming a C

shape, followed by flexion and adduction of

extremities; legs may weakly flex; infant may

cry; disappears after age 3-4 months.

10Page 10

• Grasping reflex: An object is placed in the

infant’s hand, and the infant’s fingers close to

grasp the object. palmar grasp lessens after age

3 months, to be replaced by voluntary

movement; plantar grasp lessens by 8 months

of age

11Page 11

• Babinski reflex: The sole of the foot is stroked,

and the infant responds by turning the foot

inward while his toes flare outward or flexion.

12Page 12

• Dancing (or stepping) reflex: If infant is held so that

sole of foot touches hard surface, there is reciprocal

flexion and extension of leg, simulating walking

disappears after age 3-4 weeks, to be replaced by

deliberate movement

13Page 13

•

Palmar grasp: The examiner places a finger in the

palm of the neonate’s hand, he neonate grasps the

finger tightly; if the neonate grasps the examiner’s

finger with both hands, the neonate can be pulled to a

sitting position.

• Plantar grasp: Place a thumb firmly against the ball of

the infant’s foot, Toes flex tightly down in a grasping

motion.
